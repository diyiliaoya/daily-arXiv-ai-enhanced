### [1] [The influence of standard relativistic effects on Gaia-like astrometric catalogs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02922)
*Gabriel Rodríguez-Moris,Sergei A. Klioner*

Main category: astro-ph.IM

TL;DR: 本文研究了未被计入的相对论效应对Gaia类天体测量望远镜所观测源的天体测量参数的影响。通过AGISLab仿真工具拟合不同不完整相对论模型下的观测数据，发现由于多观测平均效应，天体测量参数误差远小于单次观测中的相对论效应，这为未来更高精度天体测量项目简化相对论模型提供了可能。


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: 随着Gaia等天体测量空间望远镜观测精度的大幅提升，牛顿理论框架已不再适用，需要广义相对论框架来描述。然而，不同相对论效应对天体测量参数的影响程度不一，部分效应仅影响少量观测，因此需要评估忽略这些效应时对最终天体测量参数造成的误差，以判断是否有必要保留所有相对论校正。

Method: 首先利用Gaia标准相对论框架突出若干最显著的相对论效应场景；随后使用仿真工具AGISLab，对一定数量源体的Gaia类观测序列，采用不同不完整的相对论模型拟合并解算天体测量源参数；最后将完整模型与各不完整模型得到的参数进行对比，从而量化因遗漏不同相对论元素而引入的参数误差，并提供简化解析描述。

Result: 研究表明，由于每个源体的大量观测进行平均拟合，天体测量参数的误差远小于单次观测中对应的相对论效应幅度。这表明大量观测的统计平均能够有效抑制个体观测中未校正相对论效应的影响。

Conclusion: 未被计入的相对论效应经多观测平均后对最终天体测量参数的影响大幅减小，这意味着未来精度要求高于Gaia的天体测量项目在设计相对论模型时，可能存在简化空间，不必包含所有相对论校正项。

Abstract: With the observational accuracy of astrometric space telescopes such as Gaia, the Newtonian theoretical framework for positional astronomy is no longer appropriate. To model all effects relevant at the corresponding levels of accuracy, a more sophisticated description in the framework of General Relativity is required. It is generally known how various relativistic effects affect a given astrometric observation. In particular, the maximal light deflection of a given Solar System body can be easily estimated for a given observer. However, astrometric parameters for a given source are computed from a series of observations of that source. Depending on the relativistic effect, the fraction of observations affected, at some given level of accuracy, by that relativistic effect can vary and also be very small. Our goal is to investigate the impact of unaccounted relativistic effects on the astrometric parameters of a source observed by a Gaia-like astrometric telescope. The standard relativistic framework for Gaia is used to highlight such effects in some cases where they are most significant. Then, using the simulation tool AGISLab, we fit a set of Gaia-like observations of a given number of sources to the standard Gaia-like astrometric model for astrometric source parameters, but using different incomplete relativistic models. In this way we obtain the errors of the astrometric parameters if one were to drop different elements of the relativity model. A simplified analytical description of the errors is also provided and discussed. We demonstrate that, due to averaging over many observations per source, the errors in the astrometric parameters are drastically smaller than the corresponding relativistic effects in individual observations. This opens a way to potentially simplify the relativistic model for future astrometric projects aiming for higher accuracy than Gaia.

Examination of data for objects launched since the start of the spacecraft constellation era shows that satellites account for most of the bright observations.
